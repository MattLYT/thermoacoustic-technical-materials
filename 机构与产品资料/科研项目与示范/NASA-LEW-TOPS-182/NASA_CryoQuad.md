# CryoQuad

Power Generation and Storage

CryoQuad(LEW-TOPS-182)

Megawatt-Scale Cryocooler Without Moving Parts for Superconducting Applications

Overview

Meeting the cooling demands of megawatt-scale superconducting systems, high-power fuel cells, and other cryogenic applications has long been limited by the mass, complexity, and capacity of traditional cryocoolers. CryoQuad, developed by innovators at NASA Glenn Research Center, is a high-efficiency cryogenic cooling system capable of operating on thermoacoustic principles, waste heat from fuel cells, or other compatible heat sources. Its flexible architecture eliminates moving parts, reduces weight, and delivers reliable, high-capacity cooling for transportation, aerospace, medical, and industrial systems. Future superconducting transportation platforms, including automobiles, maritime vessels, and aircraft, promise major gains in energy efficiency and range. However, superconductors only exhibit zero electrical resistance at extremely low temperatures (below 50 K). Without effective cryogenic cooling, their performance rapidly degrades, erasing efficiency benefits. CryoQuad provides the scalable, reliable cooling required to maintain superconductivity under operational loads and unlock the full performance potential of these advanced platforms.

The Technology

Traditional cryogenic cooling methods, such as pulse-tube and reverse Brayton cryocoolers, are constrained by moving parts, high mass, and limited cooling capacity. Their mechanical complexity increases maintenance needs and reduces reliability, while their bulk adds significant weight penalties to mobile platforms. Although these systems can achieve kilowatt-scale cooling, they struggle to maintain stable, efficient performance under the continuous, high thermal loads required for megawatt-scale superconducting propulsion. The CryoQuad was developed specifically to address these shortcomings, delivering a lighter, more reliable, and higher-capacity solution tailored for superconducting electric propulsion systems. The novel design utilizes four thermoacoustic Stirling heat engines arranged a quarter wavelength apart in a quad loop configuration. This configuration – wherein each engine has high-power acoustic energy pulled off via power pulse-tube coolers – allows for rapid acoustic wave amplification without moving parts (e.g., pistons, turbines, pumps) or electricity. Importantly, the innovative design eliminates the need for large linear piston generators and large recuperator heat exchangers – two features common in megawatt-scale cryocooling pressure systems today – significantly reducing the overall system mass and complexity. While designed for use with liquid Helium, CryoQuad can utilize a variety of fluids depending on the required cryogenic temperatures. CryoQuad has the potential to be used in superconducting electric aircraft, other advanced propulsion systems, in-space cryogenic fluid management, cryosurgical cancer treatment probes, MRI systems, cryogenic cooling and packaging systems for superconducting electronics, space fuel depots, and other power applications. CryoQuad is available for patent licensing.

Benefits

- No Moving Parts: Increases reliability, reduces maintenance, and eliminates vibration for sensitive applications.
- Flexible Heat Source Integration: Operates using thermoacoustic principles, waste heat, fuel cells, or other compatible sources.
- Lightweight: Compact quad-loop design without thermal recuperation lowers mass for aerospace and mobile systems.
- No Electrical Requirements: Does not require electricity or controls, reducing electromagnetic interference (EMI) and simplifying integration.
- Low-Cost: Simple architecture cuts manufacturing and upkeep expenses, making megawatt-scale cooling more affordable.

Applications

- Electric Aircraft: Maintains superconducting propulsion systems at optimal temperatures, enabling higher efficiency and longer range in next-generation aircraft.
- Electric Vehicles: Provides compact, high-capacity cooling for superconducting drivetrains, improving performance without significant weight increase.
- Aerospace Cryogenics: Provides reliable, lightweight cooling for in-space cryogenic fluid management and long-duration storage in satellites, spacecraft, and space fuel depots.
- Resonance Imaging Systems: Offers a smaller, quieter, and more energy-efficient alternative to conventional cooling systems in MRI machines, improving patient and operator experience.
- Data Centers: Integration with acoustic tube systems could deliver targeted, high-capacity cooling per rack or server, reducing thermal hotspots and improving energy efficiency.
